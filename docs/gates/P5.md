# P5 gate — adaptive runtime

**Gate:** on a held-out mixed workload, adaptive K beats the best single
fixed K by ≥ 5%.

**Result: FAIL, twice. Not loosened.** The gate is structurally unreachable
on this hardware with this draft, and the traces show why.

## The runs (2026-09-16, `acceptrate bench adaptive`, `traces/p5`)

36 held-out prompts (6 per tag, seed 0, the split fixed before any
measurement), 200 tokens, 8B-4bit target + 1B-4bit draft, arms interleaved
per prompt: adaptive vs every fixed K ∈ 1…8.

| attempt | scheduler | adaptive | best fixed | gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (15:14–15:57Z) | corrected argmax, no hysteresis | 37.48 tok/s | K=2: 38.01 | **−1.40%** |
| 2 (15:58–16:41Z) | + 3% switching hysteresis | 37.69 tok/s | K=2: 38.12 | **−1.14%** |

Fixed arms, attempt 1: K1 33.16 · K2 38.01 · K3 34.38 · K4 31.55 · K5 27.59 ·
K6 24.00 · K7 23.86 · K8 21.47 tok/s.

## What the scheduler did

It converged. In attempt 1 it chose K=2 on 75% of windows (chat 77%, code
93%, json 84%, prose 62%, reason 85%, summarize 82%), K=1 on 16%, K=3–4 on
3%. Per tag it landed within 1–2 tok/s of the fixed K=2 arm. The loss came
from the K=1 excursions (α dipped to 0.70 there) and the cost of the
switches; hysteresis halved the switches and recovered 0.3 points.

## Why no per-generation policy can pass

From the fixed arms of the same run, the best fixed K *per prompt* (an
oracle that knows each held-out prompt's optimum in advance):

- median gain over fixed K=2: **0.0%**, mean +0.03%, max +1.2%
- 34 of 36 prompts have K=2 as their own optimum; the other two K=4

So the target is not "pick the right K for each prompt" — there is only
one right K. This is a direct consequence of the P4 finding: verifying K+1
tokens costs 1.05 plain steps at K=2 and 1.30 at K=3 on the M4, so the
25% jump in verify cost between 2 and 3 outweighs any acceptance gain from
α = 0.70 (chat) to α = 0.93 (json). The brief's premise — "the optimum
moves with α, so re-pick K as you go" — holds for the closed form with
v = 1 and does not hold once v(K) is measured on this chip.

A per-window policy cannot escape this either: the within-response α
swings the brief anticipated (high in a code block, low at a sentence
boundary) move the corrected optimum between K=2 and K=3, where the two
predicted speedups differ by under 1%.

## What the adaptive runtime is still for

- It finds K=2 on its own from priors alone, on the first window, on any
  workload — the machine never needs to be told.
- On a machine where v(K) is flatter (more bandwidth, or a smaller target),
  the same scheduler will pick deeper K; `acceptrate calibrate` measures
  v(K) into the profile. That is the experiment the 3050 Ti backend would
  run.
- It never loses to plain decoding here: every arm at K ≤ 4 beats
  baseline on every tag.

P6 and P7 were completed with this gate on record as failed; nothing
downstream consumes the adaptive result. Overruling this is the owner's
call.
