## The deviation is the Apple Silicon story

The brief's equation, speedup = (1 − α^(K+1)) / ((1 − α)(Kc + 1)), assumes
that verifying K+1 tokens in one target pass costs the same as generating
one token ("checking is nearly free when you were never compute-bound").
Measured on the M4, relative to a 44.3 ms plain decode step, one verify
pass costs:

| K | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v(K) = verify / plain step | 1.00 | 1.05 | 1.30 | 1.57 | 1.98 | 2.43 | 2.48 | 2.96 |

It is free up to two tokens and then grows almost linearly: a 4-bit 8B
model on a base M4 leaves the bandwidth-bound regime around N ≈ 3 rows per
forward. v(K) is a property of the target on this hardware — the three
drafts agree on it to ~1%. The draft's per-token cost, by contrast, is flat
in K: 8.3 ms (1B-4bit), 14.4 ms (1B-8bit), 20.3 ms (3B-4bit), i.e. c = 0.19
/ 0.33 / 0.46 of a plain step.

The corrected model keeps the brief's numerator and measures the whole
denominator: **speedup = (1 − α^(K+1)) / ((1 − α) · v(K) · (Kc + 1))**, with
c the draft cost per token relative to the verify pass (as logged per
window) and v the verify pass relative to the plain step. Nothing is fitted
to the outcome: α, c and v are all per-cell measurements from the trace.

First attempt with this model scored R² = 0.32 because of a units slip
(K·c + v); the corrected denominator is what passes. Recorded rather than
amended.

57 of 192 speculative cells lose. K=2 is the best fixed depth almost
everywhere — a direct consequence of v(K): beyond two drafted tokens the
verify pass itself eats the harvest. Full per-cell table:
`docs/figures/cells.md`; per-draft heatmaps and the predicted-vs-measured
scatter under `docs/figures/`.

## Consequence for the runtime

The adaptive scheduler must optimise the corrected form, not the brief's:
with v = 1 it would pick K = 6–8 for structured text and lose. v(K) joins
the machine profile (`acceptrate calibrate` measures it) with the M4 table
above as the default.
